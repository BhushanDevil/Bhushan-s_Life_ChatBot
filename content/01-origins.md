# Origins

I grew up in Bandra, Mumbai: busy streets, neighbourhood friendships and a mix of cultures. Growing up there taught me to adapt and to get along with all kinds of people. As a kid I was curious and wanted to know how things worked rather than just accept them.

My family taught me a few things that still shape how I work. Not everything is in my control, so instead of overthinking I focus my energy on what I can change. Never hesitate to do good for someone. Treat people nicely and respect everyone regardless of position or background, because that is how you earn respect. Don’t carry unnecessary tension.

My interest in computers started in 2011, when I was around eleven. My father bought our family’s first computer with an internet connection, and it felt like a whole new world. I explored settings, searched, and tried to figure things out myself instead of asking someone else. Soon my classmates saw me as the computer person. IT became my strongest subject and I scored some of my highest marks in it.

A small memory that shows who I am: I became the unofficial computer-help person among my friends. When a classmate was stuck, I would work through the problem with him until we solved it, instead of just handing over the answer. After that, every small computer problem somehow became my job. I joked that I should charge consultation fees. I honestly enjoyed it, because it felt good when someone came with a problem and left with a solution.
